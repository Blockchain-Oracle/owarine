The news feed is a dense list of rows: a lead story, then the wire underneath, each with a source, an age and a sentiment tone shown as a thin colored edge on the row. Every row that names a stock carries an asset-mark chip with a `$TICKER` cashtag, so you can see at a glance which tickers a headline touches. Headlines come from Finnhub and need a Finnhub key on the server; without one the page shows its empty state rather than an old story.

The same row shape (source, age, sentiment edge, cashtag chips) is reused across the news page, the activity feed and the ticker hub, so a headline reads the same wherever it appears. On narrow screens the sentiment tone moves from the row's left edge into the meta line, so headline titles keep their full width.

## Reading sentiment

The tone is a small colored mark plus a word (not just a bare color), so it doesn't rely on color alone to carry meaning. Sentiment is a keyword read of the headline's language, not a trading signal — treat it the way you'd treat any single news source's slant, not as a settlement input. Nothing about a Window's Up/Down result depends on sentiment; only the signed price print at each boundary does.

The ticker strip's **crowd flow** is different: the Up share of calls people chose to publish in the last hour, shown only when at least five people published. Calls kept private are never counted.

## Following a cashtag

Choosing a `$TICKER` chip takes you to that ticker's own hub, where the same news rows, its live price and its open Windows sit together.

## A trader's profile

A profile at `/u/<address>` shows only the calls that seat chose to publish: their settled record, badges, open published calls and takes. Anything it kept private stays private. Your own history is read only under your current lease, so a recycled seat never shows you a previous visitor's calls. Profiles have not yet been run end to end on a Canton sandbox.
