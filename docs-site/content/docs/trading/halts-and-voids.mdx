---
title: "Halts and voids"
description: "What it means when a Window can't settle cleanly, and what you get back."
---

Most Windows settle cleanly: at least 2 of the 3 oracle parties post both the opening and the closing print, and their prices agree within 1%. When that doesn't happen, Owarine voids the Window rather than guessing — a void returns every stake and every fee, so nobody is worse off for a price problem that wasn't theirs.

## The void reasons

- **Missing print** — no oracle posted a valid price for a boundary before its deadline. A source that had nothing to read at the boundary ends here; a point that was never read is never attested.
- **Quorum not met** — some oracles posted, but fewer than the quorum of 2.
- **Resolver absent** — a quorum existed, but the resolver did not record it before the deadline.
- **Source disagreement** — the oracle prices were further apart than the Series allows (more than 1% of their median on the price lanes), so the Window voided rather than picking one. An event whose committee answers both YES and NO voids the same way.

The ledger lets each Window resolve or void exactly once, and the proof page names the specific reason.

## "Trading halted" vs. "Signed price stale"

The app only shows **"Trading halted"** for a real halt signal: a tokenized stock's issuer flagging it halted. A stopped price source shows the more accurate **"Signed price stale"** instead. The venue's halt watcher never signs anything: while an asset is halted, no new Window lists for it, the market maker pulls its prices and the ticket takes no new calls. A Window that is already open is left alone and settles or voids on its own prints. A lane no source can sign at all reads **paused: no signed source**, with the reason.

No licensed halt feed exists, so a lane is halted when the signed price it settles on stops being printable. The watcher judges each lane on **its own source**, the one named on its receipts, and on nothing else:

| Lane | Watched on | Halted when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, META, AMZN, GOOGL | RedStone | The newest data package is more than 60 seconds old. |
| QQQ, VOO | Alpaca (last IEX trade) | The newest trade is more than 120 seconds old. |
| TSLAx, NVDAx, SPYx, QQQx | Jupiter Price v3, and the issuer's own flag | Three price reads in a row fail, or the issuer marks the token halted (**Trading halted**). |
| Eight pre-IPO names, five baskets | PreStocks | The newest catalogue read is more than 60 seconds old. |
| BTC, ETH | None | Never halted: they settle on three exchanges' 1-minute closes, and a missing or disagreeing print voids the Window instead. |

Every reason except the issuer's flag reads **Signed price stale**. The stock lanes are watched while the exchange is open, 09:30 to 16:00 ET, and the xStock, pre-IPO and basket lanes at every hour. A change needs two checks in a row, five seconds apart, so one late price never flips a lane; the issuer's flag counts at once. Alpaca gets the longest limit on purpose: IEX is a single venue, and a quiet minute in VOO is normal. A print may use a trade up to 5 minutes old, and the limit leaves room for that: a Window listed just inside it still finds a trade to print from. No lane settles on Pyth on Canton, so the watcher makes no Pyth request.

## What you get back from a void

Your stake and your fee, in full — a void is a wash, not a loss. The venue's settler pays it back to your seat the same way it pays a settled Window; see <AppLink href="/portfolio">Portfolio</AppLink>. A voided Boost or short also returns your stake.
