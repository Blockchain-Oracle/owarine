title: "Ops and the projector"
description: "The off-chain actors that run the venue's clock, prices and settlement, and the projector the app reads from."
---

Nothing in `services/ops` holds a visitor's key. Its actors act as the venue's own parties — the venue, the resolver and the three oracles — each a single writer, run from one process. Every command carries a fixed id (`open:<series>:<index>`, `print:<oracle>:<T>`, `resolve:<terms>`), so a retry never does the same thing twice.

| Actor | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Roller | Opens each Window on its Series' cadence with `Series_OpenWindow`. Today's lanes are BTC and ETH Windows on a 5-minute cadence and a 1-minute demo cadence. |
| Oracle feeders | Three oracle parties, one per exchange (Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp). At each minute boundary each posts the close of its exchange's 1-minute candle as a `PriceQuote`. The raw response is archived byte for byte, and its hash goes on the quote. |
| Resolver | Records the opening print and, after the close, resolves or voids the Window from the oracle quotes (a quorum of 2 of the 3 oracles; quotes more than 1% apart void the Window). |
| Pricer | Publishes the venue's price ladder for each Window. It writes nothing to the ledger. |
| Issuer | Turns a seat's request into a firm `Quote` at a ladder price, from one of the venue's cash shards. |
| Sweeper and rebalancer | Expire quotes nobody accepted and keep the venue's cash shards the right size. |
| Netting and settler | Merge the venue's matching positions, then settle each resolved Window's legs in batches with `Desk_SettleBatch`. |
| Seat funding and drain | Fund a newly leased seat with demo credits; when a seat is released, withdraw its open quotes, close its open legs at cost and sweep its cash. |
| Reserve reporter | Reports what the venue holds against what it owes. |

## The projector

The app does not read the ledger row by row. The **projector** follows the venue's stream of ledger updates and writes them into database tables: Windows, prints, quotes, positions, resolutions. The web app's `/api/index` routes read those tables. It is the venue's view as counterparty, and the screens say so. A rebuild replays the ledger into a fresh database and must match the live one. Leaderboards and takes read only calls their owners chose to publish.

Your own positions and cash are read from the ledger **as your seat's party**, so the app shows you exactly what Canton lets your party see.

## Always on, even when the market is closed

`/markets` never goes blank: the crypto Windows trade around the clock, and a last known reading is shown with its age rather than hidden. [Sessions and lanes](/trading/sessions-and-lanes) describes the board.

## What this buys you, and what it doesn't

An ops actor can open a Window, post a print or settle — it cannot spend your credits, accept a quote for you or resolve a Window alone: resolution needs the resolver's choice and an oracle quorum, and the tests prove the venue cannot confiscate a position. If ops is down, no new Windows open; a Window whose prints never arrive voids, and after its refund time you can take your stake back yourself. [Status](/help/status) shows the running actors.
