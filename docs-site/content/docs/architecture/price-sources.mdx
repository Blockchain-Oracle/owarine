title: "Price sources and signed prints"
description: "Three oracle parties post each boundary price; a quorum resolves the Window, and every print can be checked on the proof page."
---

A Window settles from its opening and closing **boundary prints**. The web app never picks a winner from a latest-price tile: the resolver party resolves the Window on the ledger, from the oracle parties' signed quotes, under the terms the Window opened with.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Oracle quotes | Three oracle parties — one each for Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp — post the close of their exchange's 1-minute candle at each boundary as a `PriceQuote`. Each quote is signed by its oracle party. |
| Evidence | The exchange's raw response is archived byte for byte, and its hash is written on the quote, so anyone can re-derive the price from the archive or from the exchange. |
| Quorum | The Window resolves only when at least 2 of its 3 oracles have a valid quote for the boundary. Fewer, and it voids. |
| Agreement | If the quotes differ by more than the Series' deviation limit (1% today), the Window voids instead of paying anyone. |
| Once | The resolution chain is single-use, so a Window resolves or voids exactly once. A void returns every stake plus its fee. |
| Tie | A close equal to the open pays Up. |

Policy versions are append-only on the Series, and a Window keeps the policy it opened under.

These are three feeders the venue runs itself. They give an honest, re-derivable price from three independent exchanges, but they are not independent oracles; the page says so rather than claim more.

**Planned:** stock, xStock, PreStocks basket and Pyth-valued lanes. Their prices would be fetched off the ledger and posted onto the same attested path by the oracle parties, with the original source named on every receipt. Until then those lanes do not settle on Canton. [PreStocks and Pyth in Agari](/architecture/prestocks-and-pyth) has the plan.
